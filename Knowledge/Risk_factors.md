# Risk factors for coronary artery disease (starter text: replace with cited guideline passages)

High LDL cholesterol lets fatty plaque build up inside the coronary arteries. Low HDL cholesterol and high triglycerides are also linked to a higher chance of artery disease.

High blood pressure strains the heart and slowly damages artery walls. Diabetes and long-term high blood sugar also damage the arteries.

Smoking damages artery walls and speeds up plaque build-up. A close relative with early heart disease raises a person's own risk. Risk rises with age.

Chest pain brought on by effort and relieved by rest, and unusual breathlessness, can be signs that the heart is not getting enough blood. A low ejection fraction means the heart pumps less strongly than it should.

A raised troponin can indicate injury to the heart muscle and needs urgent medical assessment. A calcium score above zero means calcified plaque has been seen in the coronary arteries.
